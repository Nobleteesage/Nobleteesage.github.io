Ogoniland land cover change, 1990 to 2023

A multi-decade remote sensing study of Ogoniland's four local government areas, tracking land cover before and after the 2011 UNEP Environmental Assessment of Ogoniland. Cloud-masked dry-season Landsat composites for three epochs were classified with NDVI and MNDWI thresholds, checked against K-Means clusters, then compared pixel by pixel and summarised per LGA.

Flood-disrupted hospital access, Yenagoa, 2022

A network accessibility analysis of how the October 2022 floods changed travel time to the nearest hospital in Yenagoa. Satellite-detected flood extent was intersected with the road network, travel speeds were assigned by road class, and iso-area analysis was run twice, once on the intact network and once with flooded roads removed.

20 to 24%

Mapping the October 2022 Yenagoa flood with Sentinel-1 radar

Radar sees through cloud, which hid much of the 2022 flood from optical satellites. I tested how well Sentinel-1 agrees with the UNOSAT flood extent over Yenagoa, using a fixed orbit, a median pre flood composite and validation blocks held out from training and tuning. I compared a plain change threshold, the same threshold on change averaged over about 375 m, and a Random Forest. UNOSAT is a coarse optical product, so the scores measure agreement with it and not accuracy on the ground. This is one local government area, two dates and one orbit.

0.49 to 0.65
Average precision of a single pixel change rule against the same rule averaged over about 375 m, with no machine learning (chance is 0.28)
0.67
Random Forest average precision over five spatial splits, only about 0.01 above the averaged rule
0.67 on a new date
Trained on 25 October only, scored on 13 October against a separate UNOSAT window (chance is 0.23)
0.86
ROC AUC at UNOSAT's own 375 m scale, the number that is fairest to a coarse reference

Testing a pretrained LiDAR classifier on forested airborne data

I tested how well a pretrained deep learning classifier (SegFormer 3D from NRCan's TreeAIBox, run through the LiDAR AI Classifier plugin for QGIS by Abderrazzaq Kharroubi) finds ground in a 400 m crop of forested airborne lidar with 771,201 points. The only labels available were the USGS ground labels supplied with the data, so only ground against non ground could be scored. I then built terrain models from the USGS ground and from the model's ground and measured the difference. This is one crop and one run on a CPU, so it says little about other landscapes.

0.92 and 0.71
Ground precision and recall against the USGS labels (mean IoU 0.65)
97%
Of the missed ground points were labelled wire conductor, ASPRS class 14 (128,484 of 132,175)
0.65 m
Mean absolute terrain error after gap filling (RMSE 2.64 m)
20.4%
Of the cells where the model found no ground were more than 2 m off

Flood risk and drainage redesign, Ughidane, Auchi

A community project on flooding caused by drainage failure. I combined field surveys, interviews with residents and QGIS analysis of drainage patterns to produce flood-risk and proposed drainage maps, compiled them into a spatial information system, and submitted a drainage redesign recommendation to Etsako West Local Government Area, which acknowledged and accepted it.
